# Before a Flood

Learn your area's flood risk using FEMA's Flood Map Service Center, and sign
up for the community's warning system, including the Emergency Alert System
and NOAA Weather Radio. Flood insurance typically takes up to 30 days to go
into effect, so it should be purchased well before a flood is imminent —
standard homeowner's insurance does not cover flooding. Households should
create and practice an evacuation plan, gather non-perishable food, cleaning
supplies and water for several days, store important documents in waterproof
containers with digital backups, and move valuables to higher levels.

# During a Flood

Evacuate immediately if instructed, and never drive around barricades — local
responders use them to safely direct traffic out of flooded areas. Turn
Around, Don't Drown: do not enter flood water on foot or by vehicle, since as
little as six inches of moving water can knock a person down and cause loss
of vehicle control. Avoid bridges over fast-moving water. If trapped in a
vehicle in rapidly rising water, stay inside and move to the roof only if
water is rising inside the car. If trapped indoors, move to the highest
level, avoiding closed attics.

# After a Flood

Wait for official clearance before returning home. Use protective equipment
during cleanup, including heavy work gloves, protective clothing and boots.
Do not touch electrical equipment if it is wet, and avoid wading in
contaminated flood water. Operate generators only outdoors and away from
windows to avoid carbon monoxide exposure.
