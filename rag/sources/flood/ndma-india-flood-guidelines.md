# Institutional Framework

NDMA, headed by the Prime Minister of India, is the apex body mandated to
lay down policies, plans and guidelines for disaster management, including
floods. The flood guidelines set out an institutional framework and
financial arrangements spanning central, state and district authorities,
rather than treating flood response as a purely local responsibility.

# Prevention, Preparedness and Mitigation

The guidelines address flood prevention, preparedness and mitigation
together, rather than as separate phases: forecasting and warning capacity,
dam and reservoir operation, and regulation and enforcement of flood plain
use are treated as parts of one continuous risk-reduction effort rather than
isolated technical measures.

# Flood Plain Zoning

The guidelines envisage that states will enact and enforce flood plain
zoning regulations, following the model bill circulated by the Ministry of
Water Resources, so that new construction in flood-prone areas is restricted
or flood-resistant by design rather than relying solely on downstream
response capacity.

# Flood Forecasting and Warning

A dedicated chapter addresses flood forecasting and warning in India,
recognising that lead time for evacuation and asset protection depends on
forecast accuracy and dissemination speed as much as on physical flood
defences.

# Response and Capacity Development

The guidelines cover flood response procedures alongside capacity
development, and require implementing agencies to prepare their own flood
management plans consistent with the national guidelines rather than
improvising a response structure during an event.
