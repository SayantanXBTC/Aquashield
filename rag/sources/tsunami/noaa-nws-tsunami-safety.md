# Natural Warning Signs

Coastal residents should treat certain natural signs as an immediate warning,
independent of any official alert: a strong or long earthquake felt near the
coast, a fast-rising flood or a wall of water, the ocean draining away
suddenly, or a loud roar coming from the ocean. Do not wait for an official
tsunami warning or for instructions from local officials when these signs are
present — move immediately to higher ground or inland.

# Evacuation

Stay out of the water and away from beaches and waterways once a tsunami
warning is in effect. If evacuation is ordered, move quickly to a safe place
outside the hazard or evacuation zone unless officials direct otherwise.
Anyone without a designated safe place should go as high or as far inland,
away from the water, as possible.

# Multiple Waves and Duration

The first wave may not be the last or the largest, and the danger may last
for hours or days. Residents should remain in safe zones until local
officials confirm it is safe to return, rather than returning after the first
wave passes.

# Earthquake-Related Hazards

Where a tsunami follows a coastal earthquake, evacuation routes should also
account for earthquake damage: avoid fallen power lines and stay away from
structures where heavy objects may fall during an aftershock.
