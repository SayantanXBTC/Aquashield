# Early Warning System

The Government of India operates a tsunami early warning system through the
Indian National Centre for Ocean Information Services (INCOIS), Hyderabad. A
state-of-the-art early warning centre was established with the necessary
computational and communication infrastructure to receive real-time seismic
and sea-level data, generating alerts automatically when thresholds are
crossed and disseminating tsunami advisories to authorities through standard
operating procedures.

# Structural Safety

NDMA's Tsunami Risk Management Guidelines address the design and
construction of new structures, as well as strategies for protecting
lifeline and priority structures from tsunamis. The guidelines urge the
Bureau of Indian Standards to roll out construction standards for
tsunami-resistant design, so that critical facilities in coastal zones are
built to withstand wave impact rather than relying solely on evacuation.

# Mitigation and Land Use

The guidelines recommend efficient land use practices, bioshields, shelter
belt plantation and mangrove regeneration with community involvement as
non-structural mitigation measures — reducing wave energy before it reaches
built-up areas, rather than depending only on structural defences.

# Emergency Response Coordination

Emergency response is coordinated through the local police network, civil
defence volunteers, home guards, State Disaster Response Forces and the
National Disaster Response Force. This multi-agency framework mirrors the
incident-command coordination model used for other hazard types, adapted to
the specific timeline pressure of a tsunami's short warning window.
