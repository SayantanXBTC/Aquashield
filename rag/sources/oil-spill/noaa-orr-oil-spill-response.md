# Agency Roles

The U.S. Coast Guard holds primary federal authority and oversees all
responses to oil spills and chemical accidents in U.S. navigable waters.
NOAA's Office of Response and Restoration (OR&R) provides supporting,
science-based expertise under the National Contingency Plan to help the
federal on-scene coordinator make informed decisions — OR&R does not direct
the response itself.

# Response Framework

NOAA scientists structure their recommendations around five questions: what
happened, where could it go, what could it affect, what harm could it cause,
and what can be done to help. This framework guides both the initial
assessment and each subsequent update as conditions change.

# Predictive Modeling

Oceanographers use computer models and other electronic tools to forecast
the trajectory and movement speed of spilled oil before and during a
response, so containment and protection resources can be positioned ahead of
the oil's arrival rather than reactively.

# Aerial Surveillance

Response specialists conduct overflights by helicopter or airplane to survey
the spill scene, estimate the extent of the oil slick, and monitor its
movement. Field observations from these overflights are used to verify and
correct the trajectory model's predictions, rather than being treated as a
separate, unrelated data source.

# On-Scene Support

For significant spills, OR&R provides on-scene specialists who document oil
types, shoreline impacts and oceanographic factors that affect containment
options, feeding this information back to the federal on-scene coordinator.
