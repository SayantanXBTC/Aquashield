# Stage 1 — Pre Cyclone Watch

Issued 72 hours in advance of expected adverse weather, this first-stage
warning gives early notice of a cyclonic disturbance developing in the north
Indian Ocean, its likely intensification into a tropical cyclone, and the
coastal belt likely to be affected. It is issued by the Director General of
Meteorology to the Cabinet Secretary and the Chief Secretaries of maritime
states, giving the longest possible lead time for initial preparedness
measures.

# Stage 2 — Cyclone Alert

Issued at least 48 hours in advance of the expected commencement of adverse
weather over coastal areas, this stage specifies the storm's location,
intensity, direction of movement, likelihood of further intensification, and
the districts likely to be affected.

# Stage 3 — Cyclone Warning

Issued at least 24 hours in advance, at three-hourly intervals as the
situation develops, this stage is the first at which a landfall point is
forecast, along with expected position, intensity, rainfall, wind and storm
surge impacts. Coastal evacuation and critical-infrastructure protection
decisions should be keyed to this stage, not to Stage 1 or 2, since landfall
location only becomes forecastable here.

# Stage 4 — Post Landfall Outlook

Issued at least 12 hours in advance of landfall, this final stage describes
the storm's expected post-landfall movement direction and the adverse
weather expected in interior districts, extending the warning past the
coastline itself.

# Colour-Coded Warnings

Since 2006, IMD has paired the stage system with a colour code: yellow
(alert), orange (warning) and red (post-landfall outlook), giving a compact
visual summary of the same four-stage timeline for public communication.
